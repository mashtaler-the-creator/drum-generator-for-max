# Techno (`techno`)

**Tempo range:** 118–140 BPM (classic Detroit sits at 120–125; Berlin dub techno and hypnotic techno run 118–131; minimal and rolling/peak-time techno cluster at 125–132; acid-era and bleep techno sit at 122–130; a few grinding/analogue-driven cuts push to 137–140. This is the mainline/classic lane — for 145 BPM+ distorted, gallop-kick material see `hard_techno`; for the 150+ maximalist lane see `hypertechno`; for melodic breakdown-and-build progressive techno see `melodic_techno`).

## Drum DNA

Mainline techno is a family of regional dialects built on the same three machines (TR-909, TR-808, TR-707) rather than one fixed grammar. What unifies them: the kick is a *tool*, not a distortion target — four-to-the-floor, half-time, or removed entirely depending on the sub-style, but rarely gallop-shuffled the way hard techno's kick is. The identity of a given techno record lives in what happens *around* the kick.

Detroit's own techno (Motor City Techno, Jeff Mills, Robert Hood-era Underground Resistance) runs a straight 909 four-to-the-floor with the snare and rimshot placed as **counterpoint that steps around the kick rather than reinforcing it**, three distinct hi-hat characters trading a sharp/soft/open interplay, and 55–60% MPC swing — Attack's Motor City Techno dissection is explicit that "interplay of the parts" creates the dynamic movement, not processing or velocity automation. Berlin's dub techno (Basic Channel/Chain Reaction lineage) strips that down to almost nothing and removes the swing entirely: kick unswung 4/4, snare/clap on a *single* downbeat per bar rather than the usual 2-and-4, an off-beat open hat, and a percussion/chord-stab loop programmed exactly on syncopated 16ths so the dub delay and reverb have room to breathe. Minimal techno (Robert Hood's *Minimal Nation*) goes the other way on swing — 55–70% MPC shuffle gives it a "jacking" feel — while reducing the *number* of elements to almost nothing, in keeping with Hood's philosophy of "one basic synth riff, a pounding kick, then modulate and subtract." Rolling/peak-time techno (Perc, Setaoc Mass, Tommy Four Seven lineage) adds a soft ghost kick just before beats 2 and 4 for a pendulum push and lets the open hat and alternating toms do the driving. Hypnotic techno (Phase Fatale-adjacent) puts the **ride cymbal**, not the hi-hat, in the driver's seat as a loud/soft 8th pulse, played perfectly straight so repetition reads as trance rather than shuffle. Acid-era techno (Armando's *151*) is really acid-house drums built to carry a TB-303 line: a 707 kick, densely varied 16th hats with a deliberately not-quantized feel, and a rimshot filling the gaps. Sheffield bleep & bass, techno's British cousin, applies a steppers-reggae shuffle to clean 808/909 hits rather than a house or Chicago swing, staying sparse so the sub-bass "bleep" motif owns the low end. Detroit's electro-adjacent wing (Drexciya, Underground Resistance) keeps mostly-straight 808 kicks but breaks the pattern with syncopated skips and heavy filtering/overdrive rather than a broken-beat kick.

Builds are functional, not maximalist: EDMProd's techno guide notes techno "forgoes" the house backbeat clap in favour of highlighting the kick, and describes arrangement in 8–32-bar blocks (DJ intro → breakdown → drop → drop variation → outro) rather than the snare-roll-to-oblivion language of hard dance. Swing is the single most genre-defining production choice in techno — it separates Detroit (55–60%), minimal (55–70%), bleep (heavy, reggae-derived) from dub techno and hypnotic techno (0%, dead straight).

## Sound selection & drum machines

**Kick.** Roland TR-909 (owned) is the Detroit/Berlin/rolling-techno default — tuned and compressed rather than distorted the way hard techno's kick is. TR-707 (owned) gives the acid-era lane its snappier, higher kick (Armando's *151* dissection). TR-808 (owned) covers the electro-adjacent and bleep lanes where a longer, rounder low end matters more than snap. Jomox Xbase09/Mbase01 (owned) suit the 2020s peak-time/rolling revival that wants 909 character with more analogue push. Sonic Academy Kick 2 (get elsewhere) is the standard from-scratch synthesis option for a modern main kick.

**Snare / clap / rim.** 909 snare (owned) pitched down a couple of semitones is Detroit's darker counterpoint voice; 808 snare (owned) with heavy swing is minimal techno's 2-and-4 anchor (Sub-Zero Minimal Techno dissection). Dub techno's clap sits on a single downbeat with a doubled layer and heavy reverb, closer to a Chain Reaction 909 clap than a house clap. TR-727/TR-505 (owned) rimshots and claves do the tribal/percussive-lane counterpoint work. Novation Drumstation / Quasimidi 309 (owned) give period-correct claps for the acid and bleep lanes.

**Hats.** 909 closed/open hat (owned) is the backbone everywhere; the Detroit lane specifically wants *three* distinct hat characters (sharp, soft, open-on-every-offbeat) rather than one closed-hat pattern. Minimal and acid lanes layer a brushy/shaker-like hat against a tight snappy one (Sub-Zero dissection). Hypnotic techno replaces the closed-hat engine with a ride cymbal in loud/soft 8ths, high-passed and sidechained. TR-707 (owned) hats suit the bleep/electro lanes' cleaner, shorter character.

**Percussion.** TR-727/TR-505 congas, claves and rimshots (owned) carry the Latin/tribal lane (see `techno_perc_01`, this style's original pattern). Elektron Machinedrum / Korg Electribe ER-1 (owned) synthesise the metallic percussion loops that sit under Basic Channel-style dub chords or schranz-adjacent grinding-techno percussion. Cowbell and clave (TR-727, owned) mark the electro-funk lineage Drexciya and UR drew on. E-mu SP-1200 (owned) 12-bit resampling gives the dub and electro lanes their lo-fi bit-crushed grit.

**Cymbals.** One crash per section change or drop; the ride is a groove instrument (hypnotic lane) more than a cymbal wash everywhere else.

**Layering / processing habits that drive sample choice.** Detroit techno favours saturation and mixdown-desk overdrive over velocity programming for its dynamics (Motor City Techno dissection: "no bus processing... raw aesthetic"). Dub techno leans on 6-bit bitcrushing, tape delay and long reverb tails rather than drum processing at all. Minimal and rolling techno automate an LFO on a delay or filter parameter to keep a repetitive loop moving (Rolling Techno dissection). Pick clean-transient samples in every lane — the swing, the layering and the effects chain are what create the genre's character, not the raw hit.

**Reference tracks.** Jeff Mills — The Bells (1997); Robert Hood — Minimal Nation (1994); Basic Channel — Phylyps Trak (1993); Underground Resistance — Sonic Destroyer (1991); Armando — 151 (1988); LFO — LFO (1990); Forgemasters — Track With No Name (1989); Perc — Wicker & Steel (2014).

## The 10 approaches

1. **`techno_perc_01` — Latin-tinged 909 percussion pulse.** The style's original pattern: 4/4 kick, offbeat closed hat, a rolling 16th shaker bed, and a conga/clave/rim conversation syncopated around the beat — 909 drums played with a Latin percussion vocabulary rather than a straight techno hat pattern.
2. **`techno_detroit_909_01` — Detroit classic 909.** Straight 4/4 kick, a pitched-down snare and rimshot placed as counterpoint (never on the kick's own steps), and hats built from a sharp closed layer plus an open hat on every off-beat 8th, all at ~57% MPC swing. Reference: Attack's Motor City Techno dissection.
3. **`techno_dub_chords_01` — Berlin dub techno chord-stab pulse.** Unswung 4/4 kick, snare/clap on only the third downbeat of the bar (not 2-and-4), an off-beat open hat, and a percussion/"chord stab" loop landing exactly on syncopated 16ths. Reference: Basic Channel-style dub techno.
4. **`techno_minimal_roll_01` — Minimal rolling 16th shaker.** Four-to-the-floor kick, layered brushy/snappy hats, an 808 snare on 2-and-4 with a ghost hit before the downbeat, and a rolling 16th shaker bed at 60% swing for the "jacking" minimal feel. Reference: Attack's Sub-Zero Minimal Techno, Robert Hood's *Minimal Nation*.
5. **`techno_peak_time_offbeat_01` — Peak-time offbeat open hat.** A soft ghost kick just before beats 2 and 4, alternating low/mid toms on every quarter, a fast closed 16th hat, and an offbeat open hat at reduced velocity — the rolling-techno engine built for an 8-bar loop. Reference: Attack's Rolling Techno dissection (Perc/Setaoc Mass/Tommy Four Seven lineage).
6. **`techno_hypnotic_ride_01` — Hypnotic ride-led.** The ride cymbal plays a steady loud/soft 8th pulse as the main engine instead of a closed hat; a closed hat and an occasional open hat add texture, and the snare/shaker trade a call-and-response figure that only appears in bar 2. Dead straight — no swing. Reference: Attack's Dark Cinematic Hypnotic Techno, Phase Fatale.
7. **`techno_tribal_toms_01` — Tribal toms.** A 32nd-note grid groove: straight quarter kick, a low-velocity snare roll with hits deliberately off the obvious 16th positions for a jittery manual feel, a 16th closed hat, and congas trading a call-and-response line. Reference: Attack's Spastik-style Percussive Techno, Underground Resistance's tribal/tom-driven catalogue.
8. **`techno_broken_electro_01` — Broken/electro-adjacent.** An 808 kick with a syncopated skip in bar 2 (beat 3 replaced by an off-16th hit), a layered snare+clap with a decaying delay-throw echo, and a cowbell/clave pair standing in for electro-funk percussion. Reference: Drexciya, Underground Resistance's electro wing.
9. **`techno_acid_909_808_hybrid_01` — Acid-era 707/909 hybrid.** A four-to-the-floor 707 kick with a light manual-feel swing, densely varied 16th hats with an open hat on every off-beat, a sparse rimshot, and a clap/tambourine fill that arrives only in the last bar — built to carry a TB-303 line on top. Reference: Attack's Armando-Style Acid House.
10. **`techno_uk_bleep_bass_01` — UK bleep & bass.** Sparse, dubwise kick and snare programmed with a steppers-reggae shuffle instead of house timing, clipped-short hats, and a rimshot/open-hat "chip" accent pair — the Sheffield lineage that gave Warp Records its first release. Reference: Forgemasters "Track With No Name", LFO "LFO".

## Risers

Techno builds keep the floor moving rather than stopping dead — EDMProd's guide notes the genre favours 8–32-bar functional sections over snare-roll spectacle. These risers (tag `riser`, 2–4 bars) use five specific techno build vocabulary moves: kick removal under intensifying hats/ride, a clap/snare roll, a long filter-and-percussion build, the dramatic "return of the kick," and a tom cascade.

- **`techno_riser_kick_dropout_hats_01`** (4 bars) — The kick holds full 4/4 in bar 1, thins to beats 1 and 3 in bar 2, then drops out completely for bars 3–4 while the closed hat and ride intensify from 8ths to 16ths to a fully accented lattice; a crash plus the kick's return on the last 16th cues the drop.
- **`techno_riser_snare_roll_01`** (2 bars, 32-step grid) — Bar 1 keeps the kick with a loose 8th clap roll; bar 2 drops the kick and accelerates the clap through 16ths into a 32nd-note roll with the snare joining for the final push, peaking at 127.
- **`techno_riser_perc_filter_build_01`** (4 bars) — A percussion loop (conga/clave/shaker) starts sparse and gains hits every bar, meant to run under a low-pass filter sweep opening toward the drop; the hat layers in from bar 2 and everything peaks in density and velocity in bar 4.
- **`techno_riser_kick_return_01`** (2 bars) — Bar 1 runs on hats, ride and clap alone with the kick completely silent; bar 2 opens with the kick's full-velocity return plus a crash — the single most anticipated hit in a techno set.
- **`techno_riser_tom_cascade_01`** (2 bars) — High/mid/low toms trade a run that lengthens each beat, thickening into a full 16th cascade in the final beat while the kick holds steady 4/4 underneath; a crash marks the handoff.

## Breakdowns

Breakdowns (tag `breakdown`, 2–4 bars) strip the groove back before a riser rebuilds it. Techno's specific moves: the kick-drop section carried by rides and toms, dub-chord ambient space, and a tension-hat bed — plus a minimal-techno reduction and a percussion-loop-only variant.

- **`techno_breakdown_kick_drop_toms_01`** — Kick removed entirely; a ride in loud/soft 8ths and low/mid toms trading call-and-response carry the section, with a soft open hat and rim ghost hinting at the return.
- **`techno_breakdown_dub_chord_space_01`** — Almost no drums: one soft kick per bar, a percussion "chord stab" that decays across two or three echo repeats, and a single open hat per bar — the Basic Channel-style pause where the delay and reverb tails are the arrangement.
- **`techno_breakdown_tension_hats_01`** — Kick and low end gone; a tight closed-hat 16th bed with a subtle velocity crescendo, a very soft shaker, and one rim ghost per bar keep the tempo alive.
- **`techno_breakdown_minimal_pulse_01`** — Robert Hood-style reduction: bar 1 is kick alone, bar 2 adds one swung hi-hat layer at low velocity — nothing else, in the "Minimal Nation" spirit of making each element count.
- **`techno_breakdown_perc_loop_01`** — The `techno_perc_01` percussion vocabulary stripped of its kick: shaker, congas and clave carry the groove alone, with the clave thinned to once per bar in bar 2.

## Swing mapping

Techno's swing is genre-defining and varies more than almost any other family in this library. MPC swing S% maps to `(S − 50) × 2 / 100`. Detroit techno sits at 55–60% (Attack's Motor City Techno dissection) → engine `0.10–0.20`; minimal techno runs 55–70% (Sub-Zero Minimal Techno) → `0.10–0.40`, and this library uses `0.2` for its jacking feel; grinding/analogue techno spans 50–65% → `0–0.30`. Berlin dub techno and hypnotic techno are dead straight (`swing: 0`) — the repetition and the delay/reverb tails are the groove, not shuffle. Bleep & bass carries a heavier, reggae-steppers-derived shuffle that this library represents at `0.18`, higher than a typical house or Detroit swing. Peak-time/rolling and acid-era patterns sit in a light-swing middle ground (`0.06–0.1`) — just enough humanization without reading as shuffled.

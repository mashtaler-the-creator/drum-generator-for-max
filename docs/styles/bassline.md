# Bassline / Niche / UK 4x4

**Tempo range:** 138–142 BPM (patterns hint 138–142; classic garage sits ~130, but pushing past 138 is exactly where speed garage and bassline live).

## Drum DNA

Bassline grew out of speed garage played at Sheffield's Niche club in the early 2000s and came back from 2015 onward as bassline house (TQD, Skepsis, Darkzy, Holy Goof, Notion, the Bru-C era). The drums are a relentless four-to-the-floor kick at 135–142 BPM with a big clap/snare on beats 2 and 4, open hats on every offbeat, and closed hats on the syncopated 16th positions between them — swung hard in the niche era, tighter and straighter in the modern warehouse style. Because the warped/reese bass carries most of the groove, the drums stay deliberately punchy and comparatively sparse: the character comes from hat accent contours, rim ghosts, conga/tambourine shuffle layers, and the genre's signature snare rushes and micro-fills at phrase turnarounds. Modern productions also lean on kick switch-ups (added syncopated kicks, dropped beats) across 4-bar phrases.

## Sound selection & drum machines

Bassline drums are the speed garage kit tightened for a Sheffield sound system: fewer elements, each one louder and shorter, with the clap as the second-biggest sound in the track after the bass. MusicRadar's two bassline-house tutorials and EDMProd's bass-house guide describe the same recipe. Per role:

- **Kick.** A tight 909-style kick pitched *up* one semitone, shortened to half a beat and faded so the resonant tail never touches the sub (MusicRadar); EQ-notched at the snare's fundamental so the snare gets room ("Kick Trick"). Modern producers layer two kicks phase-aligned, clip and resample them, then add a tiny fade-in to tame the click (EDMProd). Owned: Roland TR-909 (the standard), TR-808 (sub-layer only if the tune has no sub-bass), Akai MPC2000 / MPC3000 for the Niche-era swing.
- **Snare / clap / rim.** Clap on 2 and 4 at around −10 dB with a lower snare layered beneath at about −14 dB (MusicRadar); modern tracks build the clap from three layers with a few ms of latency between them for looseness (EDMProd). Snare rushes want a short, tight snare that stays clean at 32nds — MusicRadar's ultimate snare-roll build uses a TR-909 snare. Rims (TR-707 / TR-808) sit under the clap for snap and carry the dark modern rim lines. 808 cowbell/clave for accents.
- **Hats.** The loud offbeat open hat is the bounce anchor; MusicRadar pairs a closed hat (attack) with a ride cymbal (splash) on the offbeats and copies the ride tail to the loop start. Closed 16ths get an aggressive accent contour and, in the niche era, real swing; "Borrowed Time" — delaying the offbeat hats a few ms — gives the lazy feel. TR-909 / TR-707 hats; Korg M1 drums for the organ-era kit.
- **Percussion.** Shaker and tambourine for the Niche shuffle, congas on organ tunes (TR-727 / LinnDrum congas), and a breakbeat pitched +5 semitones with its kicks removed, buried at −30 dB for ghost-snare shuffle (breakbeat libraries).
- **Cymbals.** Crash on drops and 8-bar markers; a quiet metallic ride as the offbeat in the dark modern lane.
- **Layering / processing habits.** Broad EQ cuts between 500 Hz–1.5 kHz on percussion so the mid-bass cuts through, parallel compression / tape saturation on the percussion bus for density, and everything short. Choose claps with a big transient and short body, kicks with no boom, hats that survive heavy bus compression. Modern layered clap/kick packs (Crucast-adjacent).
- **Reference tracks.** T2 feat. Jodie Aysha "Heartbroken" (2007); DJ Q feat. MC Bonez "You Wot!" (2008); H "Two" O feat. Platnum "What's It Gonna Be" (2008); TQD "Vibsing Ting" (2016); Holy Goof "Getting Down" (2016); Skepsis "Goes Like" (2018).

## The 10 approaches

1. **`bassline_niche_organ_bounce`** — The classic mid-2000s Niche-club blueprint: strict 4x4 kick, big clap on 2/4, offbeat open hats with skippy syncopated closed hats between them, heavy swing (0.18), shaker entering in bar 2. Reference: T2 "Heartbroken", DJ Q "You Wot", Jamie Duggan's Niche mixes.
2. **`bassline_speed_garage_shuffle`** — Proto-bassline speed garage: 4x4 with low-velocity double-kick pickups before downbeats, snare backbeat with an end-of-bar ghost, the heaviest swing in the set (0.26). Reference: Double 99 "Ripgroove", DJ Zinc's speed garage sets, early Niche dubs.
3. **`bassline_warehouse_stomp`** — Modern bassline house minimalism: near-straight (swing 0.05), hard kick, huge clap, one dominant offbeat open hat and almost nothing else, leaving the groove to the bass. Reference: TQD "Vibsing Ting", Skepsis "Goes Like", Holy Goof.
4. **`bassline_skippy_hat_carpet`** — Hat-language study: a full 16th closed-hat carpet with an aggressive accent contour, offbeat gaps left so the open hat rings and gets choked by the next closed hit; rim layered under the clap. Reference: DJ Q, Flava D's skippy 4x4 work.
5. **`bassline_kick_flex_phrase`** — Four-bar phrase built on kick grammar: two strict 4x4 bars, bar 3 adds a syncopated "and-of-4" kick, bar 4 drops the beat-3 kick for a tom-marked gap and a double-16th pickup. Reference: modern switch-up phrasing across Darkzy / Bru-C era tracks.
6. **`bassline_niche_conga_bounce`** — Niche organ-tune percussion palette: kick and clap kept simple while syncopated congas and offbeat tambourine carry the shuffle (swing 0.2). Reference: the Latin-tinged tops on Jamie Duggan-era organ bassline.
7. **`bassline_micro_rush_roller`** — 32nd-grid roller: straight 4x4 with the genre's signature 32nd-note snare micro-rushes stitched into each bar turnaround, small into bar 2 and a full crescendo into the loop. Reference: Notion, Skepsis drop sections.
8. **`bassline_dark_rim_roller`** — Dark modern lane: a quiet metallic ride holds the offbeat instead of an open hat, a syncopated rim line becomes the lead groove voice, snare+clap layered on the backbeat. Reference: Notion, Darkzy's darker instrumentals.
9. **`bassline_organ_tom_bump`** — Organ-era tom bounce: the Korg M1-style organ stab gets a low-tom shadow the way the bumpy-organ garage lineage does it — strict 4x4 kick, clap on 2/4, a tom answering each organ stab off the beat, hat carpet kept lighter than the niche-organ or conga grooves so the tom has room. Reference: the Korg M1 organ preset "rubbery bounce" of classic Niche-era production.
10. **`bassline_virji_crossover`** — Sammy Virji-style bassline/UKG crossover ("NUKG"): bassline's low-end aggression — hard 4x4 kick, huge clap — dressed in genuine UK garage swing (0.2) and an ascending rim-shot run instead of the straighter modern-warehouse hats. Reference: Sammy Virji's bassline-to-garage evolution.

## Fills

1–2 one-bar turnarounds (tag `fill`), auto-injected into grooves of the same style every N bars.

- **`bassline_fill_snare_rush`** — One-bar turnaround: kick holds the first half, then a 16th-into-32nd snare rush crescendos across beats 3–4. The standard 4/8-bar transition in both niche and modern sets.
- **`bassline_fill_clap_stutter`** — One-bar MC-friendly turnaround: half a bar of groove, then the clap stutters in syncopated pairs over a falling tom line with the final kick dropped so the next downbeat snaps harder.

## Risers

Build-ups (tag `riser`, 2–4 bars). The bassline build is the snare rush: 8ths → 16ths → 32nds with velocity climbing to 127, open hats getting louder on every offbeat, the clap growing extra hits, and either the kick running all the way (niche era) or dropping out for the last bar so the returning kick+bass slams (modern). Several end with a deliberate silence gap.

- **`bassline_riser_snare_rush_4bar_01`** (4 bars, swing 0.1) — Modern build: 4/4 kick for three bars, snare from silence → 8ths → 16ths, kick out in bar 4 while the rush peaks at 127; crash marks bar 4.
- **`bassline_riser_32nd_micro_rush_01`** (2 bars, 32 steps) — 16ths → 32nds micro-rush with the kick thinning to beats 1 and 3, ramping to 127 and stopping dead for the last two 32nds.
- **`bassline_riser_clap_stutter_climb_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.15) — Niche 4/4 bar, then accelerating clap stutter pairs over a low-mid-high tom climb, last kick dropped, single 127 snare on the final 16th.
- **`bassline_riser_niche_organ_build_01`** (4 bars, swing 0.18) — Niche-era build that keeps the floor: kick 4/4 throughout, shaker (bar 2) and tambourine (bar 3) enter, clap adds 8th flams, hats double, swung snare roll crests at 127 in bar 4.
- **`bassline_riser_kick_dropout_gap_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.08) — Warehouse stomp, then no kick: a military "ratatat" 16th snare roll with rests peaks at 127 on beat 4 followed by three empty 16ths before the drop.

## Breakdowns

Stripped sections (tag `breakdown`, 2–4 bars): the kick is gone or reduced to one soft hit, hats / shakers / congas / rims carry the pulse, and the clap is rationed.

- **`bassline_breakdown_hat_carpet_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.14) — Skippy 16th hat carpet with offbeat gaps for the open hat, rim ghosts, clap only in bar 2; no kick.
- **`bassline_breakdown_offbeat_float_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.05) — Modern warehouse: only the dominant offbeat open hat survives, a couple of 'a' closed hats, one soft downbeat kick, one clap.
- **`bassline_breakdown_conga_niche_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.2) — Congas and offbeat tambourine carry the Latin shuffle; claps at decoration level except the final beat-4 accent; no kick.
- **`bassline_breakdown_rim_ride_01`** (2 bars, swing 0.08) — Dark modern lane: quiet ride offbeats, syncopated rim lead, low tom on the bar-2 downbeat, layered snare+clap on beat 4 of bar 2 only.
- **`bassline_breakdown_shaker_tease_01`** (4 bars) — Contoured shaker bed, hats creeping in from bar 3, open hat and a sparse kick (beats 1 and 3) returning in bar 4 with claps on 2 and 4 — the hand-off into a riser.

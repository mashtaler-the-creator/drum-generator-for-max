# Trance (`trance`)

**Tempo range:** 136–140 BPM for the classic/uplifting/progressive core this style targets, in the context of the 2020s trance revival (the wider trance family runs broader: Mixgraph's BPM analysis puts progressive trance's median around 125–130, and progressive-uplifting around 132–136, while classic uplifting sits at 138–140 — this style guide covers the 136–140 uplifting/classic end specifically, since that pocket is where the 909-driven, offbeat-hat, snare-roll-build vocabulary below is most consistent).

## Drum DNA

Trance's drum pattern is one of dance music's most stable templates, and the 2020s revival (Damaged, Pure Trance, FSOE Recordings, and the new generation of producers — Allen Watts, Mark Sherry, Will Atkinson, Solis & Sean Truby — pushing the 138 BPM uplifting tradition forward) keeps it essentially intact: a straight **909 four-on-the-floor kick** with no swing, **closed hats running the full 16th grid**, and — the single most load-bearing detail — an **offbeat open hat** on every 8th "and," choked by the next closed-hat hit, which is "particularly important in uplifting trance" for pushing the track forward. Clap or snare lands on 2 and 4. A crash marks bar 1 (and often the start of every 8-bar phrase). The ride, where used, functions as a "groove element, not a wash" — steady 8ths with a loud/soft alternation, an alternative engine to the hat wall rather than a cymbal decoration.

What genuinely separates trance from its neighbours is not the drop skeleton above but **the build-up**, which the genre treats as a compositional set piece in its own right rather than a functional transition. Myloops' bar-by-bar breakdown analysis lays out a 32-bar arc: bars 1–8 "clear the room" (kick out, only a crash and sustained pad), bars 9–16 "tease the melody" (a gated, chopped pad and fragmented lead line), bars 17–24 the "emotional peak" (the full lead statement, drums stepping back), and bars 25–32 "rebuild to drop" — where the snare roll actually starts, progressing from half notes to quarters to 8ths to 16ths, landing on a crash on the downbeat of the drop. A well-built trance riser stacks multiple layers (a tonal supersaw sweep, a bandpassed noise sweep, and the rhythmic snare-roll/uplifter layer) with their peaks deliberately staggered across the last 4–8 bars rather than all climaxing together, and the reverse-cymbal swell — "the single most reliable arrival cue in the genre" — usually starts around 4 bars out and peaks exactly on the drop's downbeat. Swing is zero throughout; the classic MPC-swing convention treats 50% as straight, and trance sits at that floor.

## Sound selection & drum machines

**Kick.** A punchy, fairly short 909 kick (owned: **Roland TR-909**) with a controlled sub so a rolling bassline has room; straight quarter notes, no swing. Live's own "Kick 909" preset through a glue compressor for punch/sustain is the standard modern equivalent.

**Snare / clap.** A single clap or a clap+snare layer on 2 and 4; when the pattern calls for it, that same hit becomes the raw material for a snare roll — half notes into quarters into 8ths into 16ths into 32nds, velocity automated from roughly 16 up to maximum, per MusicRadar's and Attack's snare-roll-build methodology, sometimes with a rising pitch-envelope on top (not modelled here — a drum-machine role can't pitch-bend, so the velocity ramp carries the tension instead).

**Hats.** Closed hat on every 16th (**TR-909**, owned) with a rising velocity contour into each beat rather than flat velocities; open hat on every offbeat 8th, choked by the next closed hit — this pairing alone is enough to make a pattern read as trance. **TR-707/CR-78** (owned) hats work for a slightly softer, more tribal-adjacent alternative texture.

**Percussion.** Congas and shaker (**TR-727/TR-505**-adjacent or **Korg Electribe ER-1**, owned) carry the tribal/Balearic-leaning grooves; rack toms (**Simmons SDS**, owned) mark phrase boundaries with short descending fills; tambourine doubles the offbeat in vocal-trance-leaning constructions where the open hat would be too aggressive under a topline.

**Cymbals.** One crash on bar 1, sometimes repeated at 8-bar phrase starts. The reverse-cymbal build moment cannot be produced by a forward-triggered drum machine role — it is approximated here as an accelerating run of rising-velocity crash/ride hits converging on the drop's downbeat, which gets the shape of the effect (quiet-to-loud, sparse-to-dense, resolving on the beat) without literally reversing a sample.

**Layering / processing that drives sample choice.** A trance riser is rarely one element: pair the drum-pattern riser with a separate tonal sweep (a supersaw opening its filter) and a bandpassed noise layer, staggering their peaks across the build rather than letting everything crest on the same bar — the drums alone are only the rhythmic third of that stack.

**Reference tracks.** Above & Beyond — "Sun & Moon" (2013); Armin van Buuren — "In and Out of Love" (2008); and, for the 2020s revival specifically, releases from Solis & Sean Truby, Mark Sherry, Allen Watts and Will Atkinson on Pure Trance / FSOE Recordings.

## The 10 approaches

1. **`trance_classic_uplifting_01` — Classic uplifting.** The canonical drop: 909 kick, 16th closed hat, offbeat open hat, clap 2+4, crash on bar 1. Reference: uplifting-trance production-guide consensus.
2. **`trance_progressive_rolling_01` — Progressive rolling.** Tighter tempo (136), thinned 8th closed hats, a ride adding roll, softer backbeat to leave room for a rolling bassline. Reference: Mixgraph's progressive-uplifting BPM cluster.
3. **`trance_16th_offbeat_hat_wall_01` — Dense hat wall.** A fully accented 16th hat wall with a hard-choked open hat and tambourine doubling the offbeat. Reference: general classic-trance hat vocabulary.
4. **`trance_909_ride_driver_01` — Ride driver.** The 909 ride in loud/soft 8ths carries the groove instead of the hat wall. Reference: Attack Magazine's "Driven 909."
5. **`trance_tribal_perc_01` — Tribal percussion.** Congas and shaker add a syncopated call-and-response layer under the standard kick/hat skeleton. Reference: 2000s tribal/Balearic-trance percussion habits.
6. **`trance_tom_accent_01` — Tom-accented phrasing.** Descending rack-tom fills mark 4/8-bar phrase boundaries without disturbing the main groove. Reference: general trance arrangement practice.
7. **`trance_vocal_soft_clap_01` — Vocal-trance soft groove.** Softened clap, gentler hats, tambourine instead of open hat — built to sit under a topline. Reference: vocal-trance production conventions.
8. **`trance_psy_driving_hats_01` — Psy-adjacent driving hats.** No open hat at all; a relentless straight 16th hat wall at the top of the tempo range (140) is the whole groove engine. Reference: psytrance-adjacent minimal-hat construction.
9. **`trance_festival_anthem_01` — Festival anthem.** Stacked clap+snare, louder hats, a second crash at bar 2 for a big-room "hands in the air" impact. Reference: 2026 festival/anthem trance scene notes.
10. **`trance_warmup_minimal_01` — Warm-up / opener.** Kick, light offbeat closed hat and shaker only — no clap, no crash — the deliberately unfinished-sounding opener pocket. Reference: general DJ-set-opener practice.

Extra one-bar fill (tag `fill`, auto-injected every 4th bar): **`trance_fill_snare_pickup_01`** — the groove holds through beat 3, then a short snare pickup (8ths into 16ths, velocity climbing) plus a crash hands off into the next phrase.

## Risers

Five genuinely different build vocabularies, matching the distinct techniques trance production guides document rather than one template restated five times.

1. **`trance_riser_snare_roll_compressed_01`** (4 bars, 32-step grid) — The 16-bar classic snare-roll build compressed into 4: quarters → 8ths → 16ths → a full 32nd-note roll, velocity 40→127, kick dropping out after bar 1, crash on the very last 32nd.
2. **`trance_riser_kick_removal_break_01`** (4 bars) — The kick vanishes for two bars while the offbeat open hat and a rising tambourine layer hold the pulse alone, then the kick punches back on the last 16th.
3. **`trance_riser_reverse_cymbal_01`** (2 bars) — A reverse-cymbal swell approximated as an accelerating, rising-velocity ride/crash run converging on the downbeat, after the kick and clap drop out.
4. **`trance_riser_double_time_hats_01`** (4 bars) — The hat layer doubles from 8ths to a 16th wall to an open-hat-doubled lattice, effectively running at double time by the final bar, against a steady kick.
5. **`trance_riser_silence_before_drop_01`** (2 bars) — A short, punchy build fills bar 1, then near-total silence for a beat and a half before a single downbeat hit lands the drop.

## Breakdowns

Following Myloops' 32-bar breakdown blueprint's own phrase structure, mapped to five distinct 2-bar snapshots along that arc.

1. **`trance_breakdown_clear_room_01`** — "Clear the room": no kick, a single opening crash, only a soft shaker keeping the faintest pulse.
2. **`trance_breakdown_gated_pluck_01`** — "Tease the melody": a rhythmic gated 8th-note hat pattern standing in for the chopped pad, sparse rim hits on strong beats for the pluck line.
3. **`trance_breakdown_halftime_kick_01`** — A halftime mid-breakdown checkpoint: kick on 1 and 3 only, one soft clap per bar, quieter offbeat open hat.
4. **`trance_breakdown_percussion_only_01`** — The "emotional peak": no kick or snare at all, just conga call-and-response and a continuous shaker/tambourine bed under the (implied) lead.
5. **`trance_breakdown_pre_riser_snare_seed_01`** — "Rebuild to drop": the snare plants half-note seeds and a soft ride keeps subdivision, the hinge between breakdown and riser proper.

## Swing mapping

Trance is straight throughout: every pattern here uses `swing: 0`, matching the genre's own convention (Attack Magazine's swing tutorial: "50% is straight timing" on the MPC/Maschine scale, and classic/uplifting trance sits at that floor). Groove comes from the hat's rising velocity contour into each beat, the ride's loud/soft 8th alternation, and the build-up's rhythmic acceleration — not from timing offsets. MPC swing S% would map to `(S − 50) × 2 / 100` if a shuffled variant were ever needed, but none of the patterns in this set use it.

# Hard Trance / Hard House (`hard_trance`)

**Tempo range:** 145–152 BPM (the 2020s UK hard-house/hard-trance revival sits at 146–152; the classic 90s Trade/Tidy/Nukleuz sound ran a touch slower around 135–145; continental hard trance — Bonzai, BXR — pushed as high as 150–160 and shares this style's DNA).

## Drum DNA

Hard trance and hard house are close enough kin that this style deliberately covers both: hard house is UK club culture's harder cousin of house (Trade, Tony De Vit, Tidy Trax, Nukleuz), hard trance is continental Europe's harder cousin of trance (Bonzai, BXR, Push, Mauro Picotto), and by the 2020s revival the two labels describe the same records. What both share, and what defines this style, is a **pumping offbeat-bass kick**: a hard, punchy 4/4 kick built to sit directly against an offbeat bassline (the "donk," the hoover stab, the 1/16th rolling bass), so the drums have to leave room rather than fight it. Around that kick sits the genre's single most quoted feature — "an open hat on every offbeat reinforces the rhythmic accent and gives the pattern that distinctive hard house drive" (The Producer School) — plus 16th closed hats kept low, and a **clap on 2 and 4 that routinely explodes into a 16th roll** (Nukleuz's call-and-response signature, heard on BK's "Revolution" and "Music Is Moving").

Where hard trance/hard house parts ways with hard techno is arrangement philosophy: hard techno "moves through breakdowns and re-entries," but hard trance embraces the classic trance arc of **long, euphoric breakdowns with dramatic builds, snare rolls and a tension-and-release drop** (Number Analytics' hard-trance production essentials), plus the trance-specific trick of a full stop — a bar of silence — right before the drop. Percussion bounces rather than washes: bouncing conga/rim call-and-response loops (the Trade-era "bounce" the genre's fans still talk about), compressed 16th clave/schranz-adjacent loops in the crossover lane, and the offbeat double-kick "gallop" that both hard trance and hard techno inherited from the same 90s source.

## Sound selection & drum machines

**Kick.** Hard, clean and punchy rather than distortion-first — a 909-style base tuned and clipped for club weight; the 2020s revival wants a bit more low-mid density than the thin 90s original but keeps the same short, tight envelope. Sonic Academy Kick 2 is the modern synthesised-from-scratch standard.

**Clap / snare.** The clap on 2 and 4 is the fixed point of the groove — a 909/808 stack for body, pushed into a dense 16th roll for turnarounds and drops (Nukleuz's signature trick). The 2020s revival layers a snare underneath the clap for extra weight (Ben Hemsley/KI/KI-style productions) rather than replacing it. Novation Drumstation / Quasimidi 309 give period-correct rave-clap and 909-adjacent tone for the classic-era patterns.

**Hats.** 909 closed hat under 70% velocity so it never fights the offbeat bass; 909 open hat on every offbeat 8th is non-negotiable — TPS calls it the element that "gives the pattern that distinctive hard house drive." The double-offbeat variant (hits on both the "and" and the "a") is a straightforward way to add energy without raising tempo.

**Percussion.** TR-727 / TR-505 congas and claves, filtered and compressed into bouncing loops for the Trade-era "bounce" approach; a cowbell (via TR-808) ghosts hoover-stab hits in gallop patterns; Elektron Machinedrum / Korg ER-1 supply the metallic, schranz-adjacent percussion loop for the hard-techno crossover lane. Simmons SDS toms handle gallop-cascade risers.

**Cymbals.** One crash marks riser payoffs and section changes, same discipline as hard techno; the ride is used sparingly as an alternate groove engine in loud/soft alternating 8ths (Push, Cosmic Gate style) rather than as a wash.

**Layering / processing habits that drive sample choice.** The kick is designed first and everything else is built to duck under the offbeat bassline it needs to leave room for — side-chaining the bass to the kick "is not optional" once a rolling 1/16th bass is in play (TPS). Pick hat and clap samples with clean transients since the offbeat open hat and clap roll are exposed, not buried under distortion the way hard techno's percussion is.

**Reference tracks.** BK — Revolution (Nukleuz, 1999); Tony De Vit — Trade sets (1990s); Mauro Picotto — Komodo (2000); Cosmic Gate — Fire Wire (2001); Push — Universal Nation (1998); Ben Hemsley, KI/KI, Daxson, TRYM — 2025-26 revival wave.

## The 10 approaches

1. **`hard_trance_offbeat_pump_01` — Offbeat pump.** The canonical Tidy/Nukleuz loop: 4/4 kick, open hat on every offbeat, low 16th closed hats, clap on 2/4 with a grace-note tease into bar 2's beat 4. Reference: BK "Revolution", Tony De Vit.
2. **`hard_trance_gallop_hoover_01` — Gallop kick.** Soft kick on the 16th before each beat (the offbeat double-kick gallop), thinning to just 2 and 4 in bar 2; muted cowbell ghosts the hoover stab. Reference: Mauro Picotto "Komodo", Cosmic Gate.
3. **`hard_trance_clap_roll_01` — 16th clap roll.** Clean 2/4 clap in bar 1 explodes into a crescendoing 16th roll through beat 4 of bar 2, hats thinning to let it cut through. Reference: BK "Revolution", "Music Is Moving".
4. **`hard_trance_bouncing_perc_01` — Bouncing percussion.** A high/low conga call-and-response fills every gap between kicks instead of a hat pattern — the Trade-era "bounce." Reference: Tidy Girls, Lisa Lashes.
5. **`hard_trance_ride_driver_01` — Ride driver.** Loud/soft alternating 8th ride as the engine, open hat pulled back to half-time, sparse closed 16ths. Reference: Push "Universal Nation", Cosmic Gate.
6. **`hard_trance_donk_minimal_01` — Donk minimal.** The sparsest approach: kick, a single offbeat open hat and the 2/4 clap, built to leave maximum space for a donk-style offbeat bassline or lead.
7. **`hard_trance_double_offbeat_01` — Double offbeat.** Open hat fires on both the "and" and the "a" of every beat, reading as far more driving at the same tempo without raising density elsewhere.
8. **`hard_trance_revival_backbeat_01` — 2020s revival backbeat.** Heavier kick, layered clap+snare backbeat, tighter hats and a hint of micro-swing (54% MPC-equivalent) the straight 90s original never had. Reference: Ben Hemsley, KI/KI, Daxson.
9. **`hard_trance_schranz_hybrid_01` — Schranz hybrid.** A compressed 16th clave loop borrows hard techno's percussion-forward grammar while keeping the offbeat hat and 2/4 clap that mark it as hard trance. Reflects the modern hard-techno/hard-trance crossover.
10. **`hard_trance_stacked_peak_01` — Stacked peak-time.** The full "three loop stack" (top loop + percussion loop + rave loop) approach: kick, open hat, closed hats, building clap roll, conga fill and ride all layered for the densest section of a set.

Extra one-bar fill (tag `fill`, auto-injected every 4th bar): **`hard_trance_fill_clap_run_01`** — kick breaks for a syncopated hit, clap accelerates from the 2/4 backbeat into a 16th run through beat 4.

## Risers

Hard trance keeps the genre's classic dramatic build vocabulary — snare/clap rolls, white-noise-adjacent hat walls, a full stop before the drop — rather than hard techno's sudden re-entries.

1. **`hard_trance_riser_kick_roll_01`** — Bar 1 normal groove; bar 2 kick breaks into a rising 16th roll to 127 while hats climb from sparse to a full wall; crash on the last step.
2. **`hard_trance_riser_clap_accel_01`** — Kick drops out in bar 2 for a hard re-entry while the clap accelerates 8ths → full 16th roll to 120; crash + kick together on the final step.
3. **`hard_trance_riser_hat_wall_01`** — Hat-density build: sparse offbeat closed hats grow into a full rising 16th wall, open hat doubles from once-per-beat to every 8th; kick steady throughout.
4. **`hard_trance_riser_gap_01`** — Build-up-and-pause: ramping 16th kick run, then total silence for a beat and a half, then one kick+clap+crash on the very last 16th — the classic trance "gap before the drop."
5. **`hard_trance_riser_tom_gallop_01`** — Gallop-pattern tom hits lengthen into a full three-tom 16th cascade while the kick thins to a downbeat and a pickup hit; crash on the last step.

## Breakdowns

Unlike hard techno's sudden drop-outs, hard trance breakdowns are the genre's dramatic centerpiece — the space for pads, vocals and melody before the riser rebuilds.

1. **`hard_trance_breakdown_halftime_01`** — Kick pulled to 1 and 3 only, hats well under groove level, rim ghosts creeping in.
2. **`hard_trance_breakdown_hats_ride_01`** — Kick removed; offbeat hats and an alternating ride keep tempo over a soft shaker bed.
3. **`hard_trance_breakdown_perc_only_01`** — No kick or hats: a conga call-and-response loop alone, rim ghosts entering in bar 2.
4. **`hard_trance_breakdown_clap_pulse_01`** — Kick absent; soft open hat and shaker hold the pulse, clap stripped to one hit on beat 4 of bar 2.
5. **`hard_trance_breakdown_echo_rim_01`** — One soft kick per bar under a rim pattern shaped like a decaying delay throw; open hat cues the return on the last 16th.

## Swing mapping

Hard trance and hard house are, like hard techno, essentially straight: nine of the ten groove patterns use `swing: 0`. The one exception, `hard_trance_revival_backbeat_01`, uses `swing: 0.06` (a 53% MPC-equivalent) for the slightly looser, more "played" feel some 2020s revival producers add versus the dead-quantized 90s original — comparable to hard techno's own "gritty analogue" ceiling of 0.04–0.08. Beyond that point the offbeat open hat starts to noticeably lag the kick and the pump collapses, so 0.06–0.08 is the practical ceiling for this style. Groove instead comes from velocity contour (hats under 70%, ride loud/soft alternation, the clap's grace-note leads) and from the interlocking kick/hat/perc grid, not from timing offsets. MPC swing S% maps to `(S − 50) × 2 / 100`.

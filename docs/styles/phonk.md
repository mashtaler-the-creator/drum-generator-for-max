# Phonk (`phonk`)

**Tempo range:** two lanes under one name. **Drift phonk** runs 130–155 BPM (guides commonly say 140–150, with some tracks pushed to 160+); **classic Memphis phonk** runs 60–80 BPM, felt and programmed half-time against its slow tempo. Overall phonk spans roughly 60–160 BPM depending on which lane you're in — there is no single "phonk BPM."

## Drum DNA

Phonk's lineage runs from **1980s/90s Memphis rap** — DJ Spanish Fly's pioneering use of "heavy bass, eerie melodies and Roland TR-808 drum machines" at M-Town clubs, then Three 6 Mafia, Tommy Wright III and the wider Memphis tape scene — through **Houston's chopped-and-screwed tradition** (DJ Screw slowing and rearranging records for that hypnotic, "slightly seasick" drag), into **SpaceGhostPurrp's Raider Klan** (his 2012 album *Mysterious Phonk: Chronicles of SpaceGhostPurrp* gave the genre its name and revived Memphis's murky, half-time aesthetic for a 2010s SoundCloud audience), and finally into **drift phonk**, which emerged in the late 2010s (with a notably strong Russian/Eastern-European SoundCloud scene) and exploded via TikTok from 2020 onward — by some accounts drift phonk now outstrips the original Memphis sound in raw popularity, and it developed a strong **Brazilian-phonk crossover** blending its cowbell/808 vocabulary with baile funk's syncopated kick sensibility.

The one constant across both lanes is the **808 cowbell riff** — "the cowbell is the signature Phonk element," used as lead melodic percussion rather than a rhythm accessory, a direct continuation of DJ Spanish Fly's original 808-cowbell-as-hook technique. Everything else diverges: **drift phonk** is a driving, high-tempo, aggressively-distorted 808 kick (clipped, near-max velocity) run either **four-on-the-floor** or, just as commonly, **half-time at a fast tempo** — "the snare hits on beat 3 instead of every beat, which creates that heavy, swinging feel even at 150 BPM" — with trap-derived hi-hat rolls and a bass either sustained or played as a distorted one-shot line. **Classic Memphis phonk** is slow, murky and half-time at its own native tempo: sparse kick and snare, generous swing (10–20%+, often described as manually dragging hats off-grid), and a lo-fi, tape-degraded texture from its cassette-tape-and-sampler origins.

## Sound selection & drum machines

**Kick.** Drift phonk: a distorted, clipped 808-style kick pushed to near-max velocity, whether four-on-the-floor or half-time. Memphis: a soft, slow, half-time kick spaced far apart, letting the tempo's own slowness do the work rather than density.

**Snare.** Drift phonk: beat 3 in half-time voicings, or 2-and-4 backbeat in four-on-the-floor voicings, often stacked (an acoustic snare for body, a metallic one for ring). Memphis: a single lazy hit per bar, frequently dragging noticeably behind the grid under heavy swing.

**Hats.** Drift phonk: 8th-note hats bursting into 16th or "32nd-feel" rolls as internal transitions, trap-influenced. Memphis: sparse, unevenly-velocity-ghosted 16ths (20-35 range) for a degraded-cassette feel rather than a clean modern kit; the 2010s Raider Klan revival thickens these into more contemporary trap-adjacent 16th patterns.

**Percussion — the cowbell mapping.** The **TR-808 cowbell riff IS the genre's hook** in both lanes, so **every phonk pattern in this set that carries the riff voices it on the `cowbell` role**. Because this engine's roles are monophonic (one MIDI note per role), the cowbell track here only carries the riff's **rhythm** — actual pitch content (root/minor-third/flat-second motion, Phrygian runs, etc.) comes from mapping several cowbell one-shots chromatically in your sampler and triggering them from this rhythm track, or from re-keying the underlying MIDI notes per hit.

**Cymbals.** Essentially unused in either lane; open hat appears only rarely, as a rare build-up accent.

**Machines.** A **Roland TR-808** (owned) is the historical and still-standard source for the cowbell riff, kick and snare in both lanes. **E-mu SP-1200 / Akai MPC60** (owned) are period-correct for chopping 90s Memphis tapes and jazz/funk samples into the genre's original sample-based tracks; an **E-mu Drumulator / Akai S900** (owned) suit the lo-fi, degraded-sampler texture the Memphis lane wants. Modern drift phonk kick design leans on distortion/clipping plugins and synthesised 808 tools (get elsewhere); phonk cowbell one-shot packs, Memphis and Brazilian-crossover alike, are the genre's real melodic-hook source material (get elsewhere).

**Reference tracks.** DJ Spanish Fly (late-1980s Memphis 808/cowbell foundations); Three 6 Mafia — *Mystic Stylez* (1995); DJ Screw (chopped-and-screwed Houston lineage); SpaceGhostPurrp — *Mysterious Phonk: Chronicles of SpaceGhostPurrp* (2012); Raider Klan (early-2010s Memphis revival); the 2020s drift-phonk / Brazilian-phonk crossover scene (SoundCloud/TikTok era).

## The 10 approaches

Five drift phonk, five classic Memphis phonk — both lanes of the genre, as the drum DNA above requires.

1. **`phonk_drift_cowbell_riff_01` — Drift half-time core.** Kick on the downbeat, snare on beat 3 (half-time at a fast tempo), steady 8th-note cowbell riff.
2. **`phonk_drift_four_on_floor_01` — Drift four-on-floor.** Relentless four-on-the-floor clipped kick, cowbell syncopating in call-and-response around it, snare on 2 and 4.
3. **`phonk_drift_808_slide_01` — 808 slide.** A sparse kick built around a long sliding 808 (tomLow layered under the kick for body), cowbell answering in short 2-note calls, hats nearly absent.
4. **`phonk_drift_hat_roll_build_01` — Hat-roll transitions.** Trap-derived hi-hat rolls burst at the end of each bar as recurring internal transitions over a half-time drift core.
5. **`phonk_drift_automotivo_crossover_01` — Brazilian-phonk crossover.** Drift phonk's cowbell/808 vocabulary voiced with baile-funk-style syncopated kick placement, reflecting the genre cross-pollination from the early 2020s.
6. **`phonk_memphis_halftime_core_01` — Memphis half-time core.** The SpaceGhostPurrp/DJ Screw-lineage boom-bap skeleton at 72 BPM with swung, lightly ghosted hats and a sparse cowbell hook.
7. **`phonk_memphis_screwed_swing_01` — Chopped-and-screwed drag.** Heavy 0.28 swing drags the snare noticeably behind the grid, sparse uneven hats, wide gaps between hits.
8. **`phonk_memphis_cowbell_hook_01` — Cowbell as lead.** The cowbell riff carries the melodic hook over a bare-bones half-time kick/snare, tracing back to DJ Spanish Fly's original technique.
9. **`phonk_memphis_tape_ghost_01` — Tape-hiss ghost notes.** A dense bed of low-velocity (20-35) ghost hats and rim taps mimics degraded-cassette texture under a minimal kick/snare.
10. **`phonk_memphis_raider_klan_01` — Raider Klan revival.** Still half-time and murky, but hats densify into a more modern trap-adjacent 16th pattern, per the early-2010s SoundCloud revival's blend of Memphis DNA with contemporary trap programming.

Extra one-bar fill (tag `fill`, auto-injected every 4th bar): **`phonk_fill_cowbell_roll_01`** — the cowbell riff rolls into a dense 16th run through beat 4 in a drift-phonk turnaround.

## Risers

1. **`phonk_riser_hat_roll_01`** — Drift-phonk hats accelerate 8ths → 16ths → a dense burst climbing to 127; kick drops out for the last two steps before re-entry.
2. **`phonk_riser_kick_roll_01`** — A four-on-floor-derived kick roll accelerates through bar 2, cowbell doubling the same acceleration underneath.
3. **`phonk_riser_cowbell_accel_01`** — The 808 cowbell riff itself accelerates from an 8th-note riff to a rising 16th run, the half-time kick/snare holding as an anchor.
4. **`phonk_riser_snare_roll_01`** — A Memphis-tempo (still slow, still swung) snare-roll build: the kick drops out while the snare rolls 8ths → 16ths to a unison final hit.
5. **`phonk_riser_gap_drop_01`** — Build-and-pause: a half-bar 16th kick+cowbell ramp, then silence, then a unison kick+snare+cowbell slam before the drop.

## Breakdowns

1. **`phonk_breakdown_cowbell_alone_01`** — Kick and snare drop out entirely; only the 808 cowbell riff keeps running — the hook is strong enough to carry a section alone.
2. **`phonk_breakdown_halftime_kick_01`** — The drift kit halves further: one soft kick per bar, no snare, cowbell thinned to two hits.
3. **`phonk_breakdown_hat_wash_01`** — Nothing but a soft, even hi-hat wash and a distant open hat, the space drift phonk edits leave for a vocal sample or synth swell.
4. **`phonk_breakdown_tape_ghost_01`** — The Memphis lane's low-velocity ghost-hat/rim texture runs alone with no kick or snare — the murky near-silent pocket between verses on original tapes.
5. **`phonk_breakdown_no_kick_01`** — Kick removed from the Memphis lane entirely; the cowbell hook and a lone snare on beat 3 keep the half-time skeleton legible without low end.

## Swing mapping

Drift phonk stays close to straight (`swing` 0.04–0.08) — its groove comes from the half-time snare displacement and hi-hat roll acceleration, not from timing offsets. Classic Memphis phonk uses much heavier swing (`0.10–0.28`), reflecting explicit production advice to try "10-20% swing or manually shift hi-hats slightly off-grid," pushed further still (0.28) in the chopped-and-screwed-style groove to simulate DJ Screw's characteristic drag. MPC swing S% maps to `(S − 50) × 2 / 100`.

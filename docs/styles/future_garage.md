# Future Garage

**Tempo range:** ~125–140 BPM (patterns here hint 125–138). Swing sits at the high end of the engine's range (0.18–0.30) because the style's defining trait is timing that never sits on the grid.

## Drum DNA

Future garage grew out of 2-step UK garage filtered through Burial's melancholy. Burial famously built his tracks in Sound Forge — an audio editor with no grid, no tempo, no quantize — placing every hit by hand and by ear, which is why nothing lands exactly on a sixteenth. Producers emulating the style turn quantize off or push swing very hard, use rimshots, congas and clicky found sounds where a snare "should" be, and treat percussion as texture: rain recordings, vinyl crackle and finger-click ticks running quietly under the beat. Velocity is fragile and constantly moving; hats are sparse; silence is a structural element. In this library, clave and shaker at ghost velocities stand in for the crackle bed, rim carries the found-sound clicks, and high swing plus the engine's humanization approximates the off-grid feel.

## Sound selection & drum machines

Burial's drums are "blocky, wooden sounds" placed by hand on Sound Forge's grid-less timeline until the waveform "looks like a nice fishbone" (MusicRadar); the genre that followed keeps that logic — rimshots, congas and found-sound clicks where a snare should be, all buried under crackle. Attack's Up-front Future Garage dissection is the most concrete recipe. Per role:

- **Kick.** Rounded low end with a clicky hi-hat sample blended in for presence, velocity-varied (first hits full, later ones softer) and manually swung. Keep it low in the mix — the kick is felt, not heard. Owned: Roland TR-808 (long soft kick), TR-909 for the Ghost Hardware-style four-to-the-floor, Akai S900 / MPC60 to resample everything at 12-bit.
- **Snare / clap / rim.** A reverbed rimshot paired with a short, spritely snare, carefully tuned to each other so the pair drives forward (Attack); Burial's snares come in "triples" (ghost-then-accent doubles) rather than flams. Rims from TR-808 / TR-909 / LinnDrum / DrumTraks (owned) are the backbone; woodblock-like clicks from Korg Minipops / Roland CR-78 (owned) for the softer "wooden" hits; found-sound clicks — lighters, keys, game sounds — get elsewhere.
- **Hats.** Two very short hats, one closed and one slightly more open but still fast-decaying, at low velocity, swung 55–65% or nudged off-grid by hand. Never a steady 16th carpet. CR-78 / TR-808 / Minipops hats (owned).
- **Percussion.** Congas and bongos as snare substitutes (TR-727 / LinnDrum congas, owned), clave-like ticks and finger clicks, a low-mixed mid-range perc swung off-grid, and one "big perc" — a heavily reverbed hit filling the empty final beat (Attack). Amen/Think slices at ghost velocity (breakbeat libraries, owned) supply the garage skips.
- **Cymbals.** None as such: vinyl crackle, rain recordings and reversed reverb tails (get elsewhere) do the cymbal's job; a slice of atmospheric noise buried in the mix supplies glue.
- **Layering / processing habits.** Minimal compression to keep the fragile dynamics; reverb on the rim, dust on everything, occasional pitch-shifting of single hits. Choose short, characterful, slightly lo-fi samples and place them by ear — quantize off, or high swing plus the engine's humanization.
- **Reference tracks.** Burial "Distant Lights" (2006); Burial "Archangel" (2007); Burial "Ghost Hardware" (2007); Burial "Near Dark" (2007); Mount Kimbie "Carbonated" (2011); James Blake "CMYK" (2010).

## The 10 approaches

1. **fg_archangel_2step** — Canonical Burial 2-step: displaced kicks around a soft 2-and-4 backbeat, clave-as-crackle ticks, four bars of hand-placed micro-variation. Ref: Burial "Archangel", "Untrue".
2. **fg_ghost_hardware_four** — Four-to-the-floor with an extra kick on the final eighth, a scattered multi-velocity rim texture instead of a snare, and an offbeat shaker only in bar one. Ref: Burial "Ghost Hardware" (Attack Magazine Beat Dissected).
3. **fg_kimbie_clatter** — Found-sound clatter: congas, rims and clave carry the rhythm like tapped objects; kick and snare are sparse and lopsided between bars. Ref: Mount Kimbie "Carbonated", "Crooks & Lovers".
4. **fg_blake_negative_space** — Post-dubstep negative space: one kick and one late clap per bar, each bar adding a single new event; silence does the work. Ref: James Blake "CMYK", "Klavierwerke" EP.
5. **fg_halftime_drift** — Half-time snare on beat three over a continuous ghost-velocity shaker bed (rain-crackle stand-in), kicks pushed to odd sixteenths. Ref: Sorrow, Vacant, Burial "In McDonalds".
6. **fg_triplet_stumble** — 2-step whose backbeat snare stumbles into ghost-then-accent doubles at a different spot each bar — Burial's documented snare "triples". Ref: Burial "Near Dark", "Shell of Light".
7. **fg_rain_crackle_bed** — Texture-first: clave and shaker weave a broken ghost lattice imitating rain-recording crackle while the skeleton stays minimal. Ref: Burial "Night Bus" mood, Vacant "Believe".
8. **fg_openhat_choke** — Breathing hats: offbeat open-hat swells choked by a closed hat a sixteenth later over a displaced 2-step kick — garage-shuffle exhale/inhale. Ref: classic UKG shuffle via Burial "Raver", modern atmospheric garage.
9. **fg_dub_techno_echo_01** — Leans on the genre's Dub-Techno lineage instead of its 2-step one: the kick skips bar 2 entirely (classic dub-techno spacing), a conga hit decays through descending "echo repeat" velocities in place of a snare. Ref: MasterClass "Future Garage Guide".
10. **fg_jamie_xx_minimal_click_01** — Ultra-minimal, click-led 2-step: a lone rim "click" replaces the snare entirely, a ghost clave ticks underneath, everything sits in wide deliberate space. Ref: MasterClass "Future Garage Guide"; Wikipedia "2-step garage".

## Fills

1. **fg_fill_rim_cascade** (fill) — Groove dissolves into a rising clicky cascade of rim, congas and toms, ending on an open hat. Found-sound tumble into the next section.
2. **fg_fill_snare_stutter** (fill) — First half keeps the 2-step, then the snare stutters through uneven ghost/accent velocities with holes — the un-quantized "dropped sticks" turnaround.

## Risers

Future garage doesn't do EDM-style risers — Burial's palette has no white-noise sweep or accelerating snare roll to draw on. These five are honest about that: they build through additive texture, filtered swells, rhythmic thinning and reverb-tail space instead of a ramp.

1. **`fg_riser_texture_accumulation_01`** — additive build: bar 1 is a lone clave tick, each following bar layers in rim, then shaker/conga, then a full swung hat pattern returns in bar 4 — texture stacking rather than an intensity ramp. Ref: Plugg Supply "How to Make Future Garage".
2. **`fg_riser_filtered_swell_choke_01`** — a literal filter-swell in drum form: the open hat grows louder and longer across two bars while a closed hat chokes it a sixteenth later. Ref: Attack Magazine "Up-front Future Garage".
3. **`fg_riser_rimshot_cascade_01`** — a quiet cascade, not a roll: rim and conga hits tumble gently with rising velocity into the kick's return, avoiding trap-style snare builds. Ref: Plugg Supply "How to Make Future Garage".
4. **`fg_riser_halfstep_thinning_reverse_01`** — the build runs backwards: bar 1 keeps the full 2-step skeleton, bar 2 strips it to almost nothing, so the groove's return reads as the payoff. Ref: Plugg Supply "How to Make Future Garage".
5. **`fg_riser_shaker_swell_reentry_01`** — a continuous shaker bed swells in velocity like a slow volume automation, standing in for a reverb-tail crescendo, kick reentering softly on the very last 16th. Ref: MusicRadar "How Burial produced...".

## Breakdowns

1. **`fg_breakdown_almost_beatless_01`** — four bars almost beatless: one soft kick every two bars and a lone ghost clave tick, exactly as the genre's guides prescribe. Ref: Plugg Supply "How to Make Future Garage".
2. **`fg_breakdown_rain_crackle_only_01`** — texture standing in for Burial's rain-and-crackle beds: a broken ghost lattice of shaker and clave, no kick or snare for a full bar. Ref: MusicRadar "How Burial produced..."; Dubstepforum "Burial Rhythm in FL Studio?".
3. **`fg_breakdown_reverb_tail_space_01`** — one "big perc" hit per bar fills the empty final beat, its implied reverb tail doing the work a busier pattern would. Ref: Attack Magazine "Up-front Future Garage".
4. **`fg_breakdown_conga_dialogue_hush_01`** — congas and bongos trade soft phrases at ghost velocity as snare substitutes, kick and snare absent entirely. Ref: KVR Audio forum "Future Garage + Burial Style Drums".
5. **`fg_breakdown_vocal_ghost_pulse_01`** — the sparsest pulse in the kit: a single rim stands in for a vocal-ghost hit once per bar, a clave ticking almost inaudibly underneath. Ref: Dubstepforum "Burial Rhythm in FL Studio?".

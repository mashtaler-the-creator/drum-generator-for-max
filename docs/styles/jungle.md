# Jungle

**Tempo range:** ~160–175 BPM (classic 93–95 jungle sits around 160–170; the modern revival often pushes 170+).

## Drum DNA

Jungle is not composed grooves — it is *chopped-break grammar*: slices of sampled funk breaks (Amen, Think, Apache, Hot Pants, Soul Pride, Cold Sweat) rearranged into new sentences. The snare carries a dense ghost-note language (low-velocity chatter around the 2-and-4 backbeat), while the kick anchors sparsely against that syncopation — typically beat 1 plus one syncopated hit per bar. Top layers (ride/tambourine/shaker from the source break, or added hats) keep constant motion, and bar-to-bar variation is essential: real junglists never loop one bar four times. Ragga jungle adds dancehall 3-3-2 kick syncopation, rim clicks and tom bounce; intelligent jungle (Bukem) softens everything into ride/shaker washes over deep sub-anchored kicks.

## The 10 new approaches

1. **jungle_amen_science_01** — 93–94 amen chop-science at 32nd resolution: bar 1 states the near-canonical Amen skeleton (kick 1 + and-of-1 + and-of-3, snares on 2/4 with a-of-2 and e-of-3 ghosts, ride eighths), bar 2 rearranges it with a displaced kick chop, a double-snare edit and a 32nd flam. Ref: Remarc, Dillinja, The Winstons "Amen Brother".
2. **jungle_think_stepper_01** — Think-break stepper: rock-solid backbeat, rotating ghost chatter, the Think tambourine 16th layer, open-hat breaths, bar-4 double-snare turnaround. Ref: Sully, Coco Bryce; Lyn Collins "Think (About It)".
3. **jungle_apache_percussion_01** — Apache grammar: the groove lives in the rolling conga/bongo 16th lattice and shaker; kick and snare stay plain underneath — the percussion is the chop. Ref: Incredible Bongo Band "Apache".
4. **jungle_ragga_bounce_01** — Ragga jungle tom bounce: dancehall 3-3-2 kick, rimshot answers, tom pickup phrases at bar ends, bar-4 snare lurched early for the skank. Ref: Shy FX, Remarc "R.I.P.", ragga jungle 94–95.
5. **jungle_intelligent_rides_01** — Ambient/intelligent jungle: flowing accented ride eighths + soft shaker wash, deep sparse kick (slid late in bar 4), brushed low-velocity ghost snares instead of aggressive chops. Ref: LTJ Bukem "Music"/"Atlantis", Good Looking Records.
6. **jungle_hotpants_funk_01** — Hot Pants break grammar: heavily swung (0.2) tight-hat funk, open-hat breath before turnarounds, kick on 1 / a-of-2 / and-of-3, funk ghosts, clap doubling the second backbeat. Ref: Bobby Byrd "Hot Pants (Bonus Beats)".
7. **jungle_soulpride_think_01** — Coco Bryce's favourite pairing: Soul Pride's dense 32nd ghost-snare chatter under a Think backbeat and tambourine eighths; bar 2 answers with an accent chop and a 32nd double. Ref: James Brown "Soul Pride", Coco Bryce (UKBM interview).
8. **jungle_coldsweat_revival_01** — Modern revival roller on Tim Reaper's stated favourite, the Cold Sweat break: bright accented ride with 16th pickups, snappy snare, kick migrating each bar over a near-swingless grid at 172. Ref: Tim Reaper, Dead Man's Chest, Future Retro; James Brown "Cold Sweat".
9. **jungle_fill_amen_stutter_01** (fill) — Amen edit-style fill: groove holds for two beats, then the snare stutters into an accelerating 16th-to-32nd roll with a velocity ramp — the tape-edit run-up into a drop.
10. **jungle_fill_ragga_chop_01** (fill) — Ragga turnaround fill: rim clicks answer the backbeat, then snare and toms trade a bouncing chop down the kit into an accented last hit — dancehall lick vocabulary at jungle tempo.

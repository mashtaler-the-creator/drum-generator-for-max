# Jungle

**Tempo range:** ~160–175 BPM (classic 93–95 jungle sits around 160–170; the modern revival often pushes 170+).

## Drum DNA

Jungle is not composed grooves — it is *chopped-break grammar*: slices of sampled funk breaks (Amen, Think, Apache, Hot Pants, Soul Pride, Cold Sweat, Funky Drummer) rearranged into new sentences. The snare carries a dense ghost-note language (low-velocity chatter around the 2-and-4 backbeat), while the kick anchors sparsely against that syncopation — typically beat 1 plus one syncopated hit per bar. Top layers (ride/tambourine/shaker from the source break, or added hats) keep constant motion, and bar-to-bar variation is essential: real junglists never loop one bar four times. Ragga jungle adds dancehall 3-3-2 kick syncopation, rim clicks and tom bounce; intelligent jungle (Bukem) softens everything into ride/shaker washes over deep sub-anchored kicks.

## Sound selection & drum machines

**The breaks.** Jungle's rhythm section *is* a small library of funk/soul breaks, each supplying its own grammar: the **Amen** (The Winstons, "Amen, Brother") for dense, splashy chop-science; **Think** (Lyn Collins, "Think (About It)", drummed by Jabo Starks) for a rock-solid backbeat plus its signature tambourine 16ths; **Apache** (Incredible Bongo Band) for its rolling conga/bongo lattice; **Hot Pants** (Bobby Byrd) for tight, heavily-swung funk hats; **Soul Pride** (James Brown) for dense 32nd ghost-snare chatter; **Cold Sweat** (James Brown) for a bright, snappy revival-era roller; and **Funky Drummer** (James Brown, played by Clyde Stubblefield) and **Tighten Up** (Archie Bell & the Drells) as the earlier-hardcore-continuum breaks that still surface in half-stepped, ghost-heavy grooves. All are owned via the 10 Most Iconic Amen Patterns pack, the junglebreaks.co.uk collection, Neekeetone Jungle Breaks and an assorted funk/soul break library.

**Chopping & timestretch.** The classic workflow samples a break into an **Akai S900 or S950** (owned), assigns individual hits — kick, snare, ride, hat, crash — to separate keys/pads, and rebuilds new bar patterns on a sequencer (**MPC60/2000/3000**, owned) rather than looping the source bar; this is "break science" — a loop becomes a vocabulary where any hit can go anywhere. The S950 (and later the **S1000/S5000**, owned) introduced timestretching, letting producers push a break's tempo up to 160–175 without changing its pitch; pushed past the algorithm's comfortable range it produces the audible pitched-up, robotic-shimmer artifact that is now inseparable from the classic jungle snare-roll and vocal sound. The **E-mu SP-1200** (owned) contributes its own signature — 12-bit resampling grit — when hats, snares or kicks are run back through it for extra crunch and dust.

**Kick.** The break's own kick stays sparse (beat 1 plus one syncopated hit) and is reinforced with a sub-weighted **TR-808/909** (owned) layer underneath, since a sampled kick alone rarely carries enough low end for a modern system.

**Snare / clap / rim.** The break's snare (Amen/Think/Soul Pride) is chopped and re-triggered as the dense low-velocity ghost language around the 2-and-4 backbeat, often doubled with an 808/909 snare or clap (owned) for extra crack; ragga jungle adds reverb/delay-treated rimshots from a **TR-727/505/707** (owned) for the dancehall skank.

**Hats.** Ride, hat and tambourine slices are usually lifted straight from the source break (Think's tambourine, Apache's ride) to keep the break's own top-end motion intact, topped up where needed with a programmed 909/707 closed hat.

**Percussion.** Apache's conga/bongo lattice, Hot Pants' funk ghost accents and ragga's tom bounce are all sample-sourced rather than synthesized; a **Casio SK-1** (owned) handles lo-fi sampling of sirens, vocal stabs and found sound for MC/ragga intros.

**Cymbals.** A single crash (forward or reversed) marks section changes; the ride is a groove element carried straight over from the source break, never a wash.

**Layering / processing that affects sample choice.** Pick a break for its groove first ("the break has to move before you start decorating it" — Magnetic Magazine); tighten transients before adding layers, EQ out rumble/hiss, use parallel/upward compression (OTT-style) to glue the chopped hits back together, and reserve heavy saturation for the sub-kick layer rather than the break itself so its character survives.

**Reference tracks.** The Winstons — Amen, Brother (1969); Lyn Collins — Think (About It) (1972); Incredible Bongo Band — Apache (1973); Bobby Byrd — Hot Pants (Bonus Beats) (1971); LTJ Bukem — Music (1992); Shy FX — Original Nuttah (1994).

## The 10 approaches

1. **jungle_amen_01** — The plain amen groove: kick on 1 plus a syncopated hit, backbeat snare on 2/4 with a light ghost, steady eighth-note hats — the skeleton every other approach here complicates.
2. **jungle_amen_science_01** — 93–94 amen chop-science at 32nd resolution: bar 1 states the near-canonical Amen skeleton (kick 1 + and-of-1 + and-of-3, snares on 2/4 with a-of-2 and e-of-3 ghosts, ride eighths), bar 2 rearranges it with a displaced kick chop, a double-snare edit and a 32nd flam. Ref: Remarc, Dillinja, The Winstons "Amen Brother".
3. **jungle_think_stepper_01** — Think-break stepper: rock-solid backbeat, rotating ghost chatter, the Think tambourine 16th layer, open-hat breaths, bar-4 double-snare turnaround. Ref: Sully, Coco Bryce; Lyn Collins "Think (About It)".
4. **jungle_apache_percussion_01** — Apache grammar: the groove lives in the rolling conga/bongo 16th lattice and shaker; kick and snare stay plain underneath — the percussion is the chop. Ref: Incredible Bongo Band "Apache".
5. **jungle_ragga_bounce_01** — Ragga jungle tom bounce: dancehall 3-3-2 kick, rimshot answers, tom pickup phrases at bar ends, bar-4 snare lurched early for the skank. Ref: Shy FX, Remarc "R.I.P.", ragga jungle 94–95.
6. **jungle_intelligent_rides_01** — Ambient/intelligent jungle: flowing accented ride eighths + soft shaker wash, deep sparse kick (slid late in bar 4), brushed low-velocity ghost snares instead of aggressive chops. Ref: LTJ Bukem "Music"/"Atlantis", Good Looking Records.
7. **jungle_hotpants_funk_01** — Hot Pants break grammar: heavily swung (0.2) tight-hat funk, open-hat breath before turnarounds, kick on 1 / a-of-2 / and-of-3, funk ghosts, clap doubling the second backbeat. Ref: Bobby Byrd "Hot Pants (Bonus Beats)".
8. **jungle_soulpride_think_01** — Coco Bryce's favourite pairing: Soul Pride's dense 32nd ghost-snare chatter under a Think backbeat and tambourine eighths; bar 2 answers with an accent chop and a 32nd double. Ref: James Brown "Soul Pride", Coco Bryce (UKBM interview).
9. **jungle_coldsweat_revival_01** — Modern revival roller on Tim Reaper's stated favourite, the Cold Sweat break: bright accented ride with 16th pickups, snappy snare, kick migrating each bar over a near-swingless grid at 172. Ref: Tim Reaper, Dead Man's Chest, Future Retro; James Brown "Cold Sweat".
10. **jungle_funkydrummer_halfstep_01** — Funky Drummer half-step: Clyde Stubblefield's original break barely states a backbeat at all — an off-kilter 16th ghost-snare pattern with the kick answering off the downbeat rather than locking to it, half-stepped and ghost-heavy rather than dense chop-science. Ref: James Brown "Funky Drummer".

Four one-bar fills (tag `fill`, auto-injected every 4th bar): **`jungle_fill_01`** (plain snare-roll turnaround), **`jungle_fill_toms_01`** (descending tom cascade), **`jungle_fill_amen_stutter_01`** (amen edit-style stutter into an accelerating 16th-to-32nd roll), **`jungle_fill_ragga_chop_01`** (ragga turnaround: rim clicks answer the backbeat, then snare and toms trade a bouncing chop into an accented last hit).

## Risers

1. **`jungle_riser_amen_rollup_01`** — Amen roll-up: a plain amen groove bar, then the classic tape-edit roll-up — the snare accelerates 8ths → 16ths → 32nds to 127 as the kick drops out, ending on a silent last 32nd.
2. **`jungle_riser_timestretch_snare_climb_01`** — Timestretch-era snare climb: voices the S950/S1000 pitched-up snare-roll effect through density and velocity instead of pitch — ghost chatter thickens, the kick thins to beat 1, then disappears for an unbroken 16th snare roll ramping 64→127.
3. **`jungle_riser_mc_intro_build_01`** — MC-intro build: the DJ-friendly progressive intro compressed to four bars, layering in ride, backbeat and shaker bar by bar until a full amen-flavoured groove and crash cue the drop.
4. **`jungle_riser_ragga_rewind_01`** — Ragga rewind: the dancehall 3-3-2 groove gives way to an accelerating rim/tom call-and-response reload, falling completely silent for the last two steps — the "hold it... rewind" pause.
5. **`jungle_riser_break_science_stutter_01`** — Break-science stutter: a plain groove bar stutter-edits into a combined kick+snare roll tightening from 8ths to 32nds, landing a crash-topped hit just before a silent final step.

## Breakdowns

1. **`jungle_breakdown_amen_ghost_01`** — Stripped amen-ghost: kick gone entirely, only low-velocity ghost-snare chatter and a soft ride wash remain, with one clap cueing the return.
2. **`jungle_breakdown_halftime_sub_01`** — Half-time breakdown: kick halves to beats 1 and 3, the backbeat snare disappears, and a sparse ride/shaker wash keeps time.
3. **`jungle_breakdown_ragga_dub_01`** — Ragga dub: no kick, a rimshot skank trades with a conga call-and-response, both fading bar to bar like a dub echo.
4. **`jungle_breakdown_intelligent_pad_01`** — Intelligent-jungle pad section: four bars of no kick, no snare — only a flowing ride wash, soft hat pulse and shaker bed, LTJ Bukem-style atmosphere.
5. **`jungle_breakdown_mc_space_01`** — MC space: the barest breakdown here — rim ticks and a whisper of shaker — with a single accented snare on the last 16th cueing the return.
